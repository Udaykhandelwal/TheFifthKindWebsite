Instagram marketing for jewellery stores: the 2026 playbook

By Uday Khandelwal, The Fifth Kind · Published 20 November 2026

Short answerA jewellery store's Instagram works when it mixes reels and carousels, shows real pieces lit properly, plans festival and wedding-season campaigns weeks ahead, runs Meta ads to buyers near the store, and replies to every DM fast enough to turn it into a WhatsApp chat or a visit. About five reels and four posts a month, plus stories, is a workable starting rhythm for most stores.

This playbook comes from making more than 25 approved reels, carousels and festival campaigns for Megha Jewellers and its collections VS Signature and Velvet Lite 9KT. You can see that work here.

The content mix

Don't make every post a reel. An analysis by Vaizle of more than 700 jewellery-brand posts over 90 days found that reels did not outperform carousels. Reels bring new people in. Carousels and static posts get saved and shared, and they make the grid look like a store worth visiting. A balanced month looks like this:

FormatPer monthWhat it's for
Product reels2–3Macro shots, try-ons, "how it's made"
Story reels1–2Owner, family history, customer moments
Carousels2Collections, "which one would you pick", jewellery history
Static posts2Festive greetings with a product, launches
StoriesMost daysNew arrivals, polls, behind the counter

Education works especially well. Posts like "Your nath isn't originally Indian" or the real history of the nose pin get saves and shares that a plain product photo never does.

Filming real pieces

  • Light for the metal, not the room. A soft light from one side brings out gold. Small hard highlights make diamonds and polki sparkle.
  • Go macro and move slowly. A slow push-in on a piece for two seconds beats ten fast cuts.
  • Show it worn. Buyers want to see scale. Ear, nose, neck and wrist shots sell better than a piece on a velvet tray.
  • Keep the design real. If you use AI for backgrounds or motion, check every frame against the actual piece. A changed stone or setting costs trust.
  • Write in your buyers' language. English plus Kannada, Hindi, Tamil or Telugu captions reach the families who actually buy.

The festival calendar

Jewellery buying in India follows festivals and weddings. Plan each campaign three to four weeks ahead, with a teaser, the launch and a reminder close to the day:

  • Akshaya Tritiya (April–May): gold buying, coins and light jewellery.
  • Wedding season: bridal sets, family stories, "the first piece she wore".
  • Varamahalakshmi, Onam and regional festivals: temple jewellery and traditional designs.
  • Dasara and Navratri: festive wear and gifting.
  • Dhanteras and Diwali: the biggest gold-buying window of the year.

Ads to local buyers

Most buyers still visit the store before they spend. Use Meta ads to put your best-performing reel in front of people within a few kilometres of the store, and send them to a WhatsApp chat rather than a website. Start with a small daily budget, keep the one ad that gets the most chats, and drop the rest every week.

Turning DMs into visits

  • Reply within the hour during store hours. A late reply is a lost buyer.
  • Move the chat to WhatsApp, share two or three pieces with prices or a price range, and offer a visit time.
  • Use a comment keyword such as "PRICE" with an automatic DM so viewers ask without feeling shy.
  • A WhatsApp chatbot can share your catalogue and book visits after hours.

FAQ

How often should a jewellery store post on Instagram?
A workable starting rhythm is about nine feed posts a month (roughly five reels and four carousels or static posts) plus stories on most days, with more during festivals and wedding season.
Are reels better than carousels for jewellery?
Not always. A Vaizle analysis of more than 700 jewellery-brand posts found reels did not outperform carousels. Use reels to reach new people and carousels and static posts to get saves, shares and a grid that builds trust.
Can AI be used for jewellery content?
Yes, for light, backgrounds, motion and lifestyle scenes, as long as the jewellery itself stays exactly as it is in real life. Every frame should be checked against the real piece.
Should a jewellery store run Instagram ads?
Yes, in a small and local way. Ads to people near the store, sending them to WhatsApp, usually bring more real enquiries than boosting posts to all of India.
